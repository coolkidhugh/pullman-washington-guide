# Pullman WA Things to Do: A One-Day Pullman Washington Guide to WSU, Food and Local Shops

*English · [日本語](ja.md) · [Español](es.md)* — by H ([@odpanda](https://x.com/odpanda))

Looking for Pullman WA things to do in a single day? This short Pullman Washington guide sums up what matters for a first visit to WSU Pullman and its college town: one campus walk, three blocks of Main Street, a handful of places to eat that are run by local people, and one Palouse viewpoint if you have a car. The full research, with addresses, maps, and sources, is in the **[Pullman, Washington one-day guide: food, fun and local shops](https://yuzicheng.org/pullman-washington-guide/)**. What follows is my curated version.

## The one timing trap: Ferdinand's is a weekday place

Before anything else, check the day. **Ferdinand's Ice Cream Shoppe / WSU Creamery is open Monday–Friday, 9:30am–4:30pm**, plus only select Saturdays (Family Weekend, Commencement, and home football days such as 24 Oct vs Boise State and 14 Nov vs Colorado State, hours TBD). It is closed 24 Dec 2026–3 Jan 2027. If you arrive on an ordinary Saturday, it will be shut. Go on a weekday morning.

Why bother? Ferdinand's sells the university's own ice cream (Huckleberry Ripple and Cougar Tracks are the classics) and Cougar Gold, a white cheddar sold in a 30 oz can. The name honors Dr. N. S. Golding, not the color. The can came out of a 1940s project between the U.S. government and the American Can Co. You can watch cheesemaking Monday–Thursday, 10am–2pm.

## Morning: a WSU Pullman campus walk

Most of the campus is free and fits in half a day. Park and pick up maps at the **Brelsford Visitor Center** (150 E Spring, Mon–Fri 8–5), take a photo at the 15-ft WSU letters, then head to:

- **Bryan Clock Tower** and the **Terrell Library rooftop**, which has a wide view over the Palouse
- **Cougar Pride**, an 11-ft bronze statue from 2008
- **Jordan Schnitzer Museum** (free, Tue–Sat 10–4) and **Conner Museum** (free)
- **The Bookie** for Coug gear and Creamery cheese

Weekday campus parking needs a permit, so starting at Brelsford makes sense. The WSU Bear Center (Terre View Dr) keeps grizzlies for research. You watch from outside the fence, and there are no set hours, so a sighting is a matter of luck.

## Lunch to dinner: where to eat in Pullman

Downtown is compact. Almost everything sits on or near E Main within about three blocks, so you can park once and walk. A few highlights from the guide's [list of 24 places to eat and drink in Pullman](https://yuzicheng.org/pullman-washington-guide/#eat):

- **Rico's Public House** (200 E Main, est. 1909): fish & chips, 20 taps, live jazz. It got through Prohibition by selling "Smokehouse Milkshakes."
- **The Black Cypress** (215 E Main): the special-occasion dinner. Dinner only, closed Sunday. Book ahead.
- **The Old European** (455 S Grand): breakfast only. Danish aebleskivers made from a great-grandmother's immigrant recipe.
- **Cougar Country Drive-In** (760 N Grand): Cougar Super Basket and hand-spun shakes. It **changed owners in April 2025**. The sale required the buyers to keep the name and menu, but it is no longer the Wagoner family's place.
- **Mela Bangladeshi Cuisine** (350 E Main, Tue–Sat 4–9pm): went from a market stall to a downtown restaurant. Good vegan and gluten-free options.
- **Paradise Creek Brewery**: dinner inside Pullman's first federal post office, built in 1930.
- **Pups & Cups** (250 E Main, closed Mon): coffee for you, and an off-leash yard out back for your dog.

One warning: **Sugar Babe Bakery's status is uncertain.** Listings conflict and recent ones show it closed, so don't plan around it.

## Shops on Main Street

Small independent shops make the afternoon walk worthwhile. **Brused Books** (235 E Main) has 100,000+ used books and has been there since 1981. **Noshies** pairs cheese with wine and craft beer. **B & L Bicycles** is the stop for the Chipman Trail, and **DREGS Thrift & Vintage** sells vinyl, film cameras, and vintage clothing. Hours vary by shop and are listed in the guide. For something to take home, a can of Cougar Gold is the obvious choice.

## Outdoors: Kamiak Butte, not Palouse Falls

If you have a car and an hour or two, **Kamiak Butte** is the best fit for one day. It's about 11 miles north, with a 3.5-mile Pine Ridge Trail loop up to 3,641 ft. No pass is needed, and the view looks out over wheat fields. **Steptoe Butte** (~35–45 min) is great at sunset but requires a Discover Pass. With no car, walk or bike the flat **Bill Chipman Palouse Trail**.

**Palouse Falls is not a local stop.** It is **83 miles away** (~1.5 hours each way), so it only works as its own half-day or full-day trip.

## Optional detour: Moscow, Idaho

Moscow, ID is about 8 miles east, so it is **a detour outside Pullman**, not part of the town. It's worth the trip on a Saturday for the Moscow Farmers Market (May–Oct, 8am–1pm) and BookPeople.

## FAQ

**Is one day enough for Pullman, WA?**
Yes, for the highlights: the campus and Ferdinand's in the morning, Main Street from lunch through dinner, and Kamiak Butte if you drive.

**When is Ferdinand's open?**
Monday–Friday 9:30am–4:30pm, plus select event Saturdays. Check the official hours before you go.

**Can I visit Palouse Falls from Pullman on the same day?**
Only if you give it its own half day or more. It is 83 miles each way.

**When is Pullman busiest?**
Home football games, Family Weekends, and Commencement. Hotels and restaurants fill up, so book early.
